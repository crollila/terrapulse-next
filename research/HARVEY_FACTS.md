# Verified facts: Hurricane Harvey (2017), Hurricane Ike (2008), Saffir-Simpson scale

All sources were downloaded on 2026-11-16 (UTC) and are saved under `research/raw/facts/`.
Every quote below was copied from the downloaded file. PDF text extraction inserts stray spaces
inside some words (e.g. "Tex as", "95 k t"); those extraction artifacts were removed, nothing else was changed.
Nothing here comes from memory. Anything not found in a downloaded source is listed under "Not verified".

## 1. Harvey: landfall (NHC)

Source: NHC Tropical Cyclone Report, Hurricane Harvey (AL092017), Eric S. Blake and David A. Zelinsky,
dated 9 May 2018 (original report date 23 January 2018, per the report's footnote).
URL: https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL092017_Harvey.pdf

| Fact | Exact quote |
|---|---|
| First landfall: place and time | "Harvey's center made landfall on the northern end of San Jose Island about 5 n mi east of Rockport, Texas at 0300 UTC that day." ("that day" = 26 August, from the preceding sentence: "...intensified into a category 4 hurricane by 0000 UTC 26 August.") |
| Landfall intensity | "Sustained winds of 115 kt and a minimum central pressure of 937 mb are estimated for that landfall." |
| Second landfall | "The hurricane then made a second landfall on the Texas mainland 3 h later, slightly weaker due to land interaction, with 105 kt winds and an estimated central pressure of 948 mb..." |
| Category at landfall | "...Harvey rapidly intensified into a category 4 hurricane (on the Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale) before making landfall along the middle Texas coast." |
| Stall and rainfall | "The storm then stalled, with its center over or near the Texas coast for four days, dropping historic amounts of rainfall of more than 60 inches over southeastern Texas." |

Note on the date: 0300 UTC 26 August is the evening of 25 August in Texas local time; EIA accordingly writes
"Hurricane Harvey, which first made landfall on August 25, 2017" (EIA, 1 Sep 2017, see section 3).

Caveats (important for the UI copy):
- "Highest figure found" means highest among the reports actually read (#8 to #14 and #22). Updates #1 to #7 and #15 to #21 were NOT read, so "peak" is not proven; say "as of 30 August 2017, DOE reported..." rather than "at peak".
- Update #9 counts refineries "shut down or in the process of shutting down" (6 + 7 + 2 = 15 by addition of the three counts in the quote; the report itself does not print the total 15).
- These are nameplate capacities of affected refineries, "according to public reports", not measured lost output. For measured throughput use EIA (section 3).

## 3. Harvey: refinery runs and gasoline prices (EIA Today in Energy)

| Fact | Exact quote | URL | Source date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drop in Gulf Coast refinery inputs | "For the week ending September 1, 2017, gross inputs to refineries in the U.S. Gulf Coast fell by 3.2 million b/d, or 34%, from the previous week, the largest drop since Hurricanes Gustav and Ike in 2008." | https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=32852 | 11 Sep 2017 |
| Utilization | "Weekly refinery utilization in the region fell from 96% to 63%, while other areas of the country remained virtually unchanged." | same | 11 Sep 2017 |
| Share of US capacity | "Just over half of all U.S. refinery capacity is located in the U.S. Gulf Coast (defined as Petroleum Administration for Defense District 3)." and "Texas, where Harvey made landfall, represents 31% of all U.S. refinery capacity, based on data from January 2017." | same | 11 Sep 2017 |
| Retail gasoline | "The U.S. average regular retail gasoline price increased 28 cents per gallon (gal), from $2.40/gal on August 28, 2017 to $2.68/gal on September 4..." | same | 11 Sep 2017 |
| Regional gasoline | "Gulf Coast gasoline prices rose by 35 cents per gallon to $2.51/gal, Texas state average prices rose 40 cents per gallon to $2.56/gal, and Houston prices rose by 35 cents per gallon to $2.43/gal from August 28 to September 4." | same | 11 Sep 2017 |
| Largest increase since Katrina | "...these were the largest weekly increases in gasoline prices since Hurricane Katrina in 2005." | same | 11 Sep 2017 |
| Colonial Pipeline | "Colonial connects 29 refineries and 267 distribution terminals and carries up to 2.5 million b/d of gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel from Houston to as far north as New York Harbor." | same | 11 Sep 2017 |
| Pre-storm context | "The U.S. Gulf of Mexico accounts for nearly 20% of total U.S. crude oil production, and the Texas Gulf Coast is home to nearly one-third of U.S. refining capacity." | https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=32652 | 25 Aug 2017 |
| Landfall date / supply uncertainty | "Hurricane Harvey, which first made landfall on August 25, 2017, has created considerable uncertainty for gasoline supply and prices..." | https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=32752 | 1 Sep 2017 |
| Restart obstacles | "Several challenges still remain in quickly restarting refineries, including loss of electricity, the ability of personnel to reach the refineries, and the capacity to gather and distribute crude oil and petroleum products." | same (id=32752) | 1 Sep 2017 |

Cross-check against our own data file (`public/data/infrastructure/harvey_prices.json`, EIA history workbooks):
- WGIRIP32 (PADD 3 gross inputs, thousand b/d): 2017-08-25 = 9358, 2017-09-01 = 6178, 2017-09-08 = 5915 (low of the window). 9358 - 6178 = 3180, i.e. the "3.2 million b/d, or 34%" in the article.
- W_NA_YUP_R30_PER (PADD 3 utilization, %): 2017-08-25 = 96.0, 2017-09-01 = 63.4, 2017-09-08 = 60.7.
- EMM_EPMR_PTE_NUS_DPG (US regular retail, $/gal): 2017-08-28 = 2.399, 2017-09-04 = 2.679, 2017-09-11 = 2.685.

## 4. Hurricane Ike (2008)

Source: NHC Tropical Cyclone Report, Hurricane Ike (AL092008), Robbie Berg, 23 January 2009 (with later updates listed on its first page).
URL: https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL092008_Ike.pdf

| Fact | Exact quote |
|---|---|
| Landfall place and time | "Ike turned to the north-northwest, and its center made landfall along the north end of Galveston Island, Texas, at 0700 UTC 13 September." |
| Intensity before landfall | "...a 40 n mi diameter eye formed during the hours before landfall, and maximum winds increased to 95 kt." |
| Track after landfall | "The hurricane's center continued up through Galveston Bay, just east of Houston, then northward across eastern Texas." |
| Refineries | "The U.S. Department of Energy said that 14 oil refineries were closed by the storm, as well as two Texas strategic petroleum reserve sites, causing rising gas prices and gas shortages across parts of the United States." |
| Ports | "...kept those ports closed after the storm for several days, leaving almost 150 tankers, cargo vessels, and container ships waiting offshore" (about the Ports of Galveston and Houston) |
| Surge | "Storm surge levels on Galveston Island and on the west side of Galveston Bay are estimated to be between 10 and 15 ft." |

EIA context for Ike (25 Aug 2017 article, https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=32652): Gustav and Ike (2008) and Isaac (2012)
"...each shutting in more than one million barrels per day of crude oil production and more than three billion cubic feet per day of natural gas production and temporarily shutting down significant pipeline and refining capacity."

## 5. Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale (for the wind model)

Source: NOAA National Hurricane Center, "Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale".
URL: https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/aboutsshws.php (retrieved 2026-11-16; the page shows no publication date)

| Category | Sustained winds (mph) | Sustained winds (kt) | km/h |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 74-95 | 64-82 | 119-153 |
| 2 | 96-110 | 83-95 | 154-177 |
| 3 (major) | 111-129 | 96-112 | 178-208 |
| 4 (major) | 130-156 | 113-136 | 209-251 |
| 5 (major) | 157 or higher | 137 or higher | 252 or higher |

Quote: "The Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale is a 1 to 5 rating based only on a hurricane's maximum sustained wind speed. This scale does not take into account other potentially deadly hazards such as storm surge, rainfall flooding, and tornadoes."
(Harvey's 115 kt first landfall falls in the Category 4 band, 113-136 kt; Ike's 95 kt falls in the Category 2 band, 83-95 kt. The Ike report page read here was not searched for an explicit "category 2 at landfall" sentence.)
